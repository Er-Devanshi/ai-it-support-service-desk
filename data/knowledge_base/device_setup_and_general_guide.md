# Device Setup and General IT Troubleshooting Guide

## Overview
This guide covers setting up new work devices and what to do when encountering unexpected or general issues. Follow each section for guidance.

## Setting Up a New Work Device

**What to do first:**
When you receive a new work computer or device, follow these steps to ensure it is configured correctly and you can access all necessary resources.

**Step-by-step setup process:**

1. **Power on and complete the initial setup.** Turn on the device and follow the first-time setup wizard. Create or use your corporate account username and password if prompted. This may take 15–30 minutes.

2. **Join the corporate domain (Windows devices).** During setup or after logon, you may be prompted to join the corporate domain. Answer "Yes" and enter your corporate credentials. This connects your device to the corporate network and applies security policies.

3. **Connect to the corporate network.** Go to your Wi-Fi or network settings and select the official corporate Wi-Fi network (ask your manager or IT for the network name if unsure). Enter your corporate username and password if prompted. For wired connection, plug an Ethernet cable into the network port on your device.

4. **Download and install required software.** Most organizations provide a list of approved software and tools you need for your role. Check the IT Self-Service Portal or ask your manager for a list. Download installers only from official sources provided by [contact IT Helpdesk].

5. **Set up email and calendar.** Open Outlook or your email application and add your corporate email account. When prompted, enter your username (usually in format `domain\username`) and password. Calendars and contacts should sync automatically.

6. **Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) for your account.** Go to the IT Self-Service Portal → Security → Set up MFA. Follow the steps to register an authenticator app or phone number. This protects your account from unauthorized access.

7. **Install antivirus and security software.** If not already installed, download and install your company's approved antivirus from the IT portal. Ensure it is enabled and set to auto-update.

8. **Back up your data to cloud storage.** Set up automatic backup for your important files using OneDrive, SharePoint, or your company's approved cloud storage. This ensures you do not lose work if your device fails.

9. **Test access to key resources.** Verify you can access shared network folders, email, company intranet, and any line-of-business applications you need for your role. If you cannot access something, submit a ticket to [contact IT Helpdesk] immediately.

10. **Change your default password.** Go to Control Panel (Windows) or System Preferences (macOS) and change your password to something only you know. Use a strong password with uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols.

**When to contact IT:** If you get stuck at any step or encounter errors, contact [contact IT Helpdesk] with the error message and which step you are on. Many device setup issues can be resolved quickly by IT.

## Something Is Broken and Urgent: General Troubleshooting

**Symptoms:**
- An unexpected problem has occurred
- You are unsure what to do or which guide applies to your issue
- You need help immediately but do not know the exact problem category
- Multiple things seem to be failing at once

**Step-by-step response:**

1. **Restart your computer.** Close all applications, perform a full shutdown (not sleep), wait 10 seconds, and power back on. This clears many temporary issues and resets network connections. Wait 2–3 minutes for the computer to fully start.

2. **Restart your network connection.** If network-related, disconnect from Wi-Fi or unplug the Ethernet cable, wait 10 seconds, and reconnect. This often restores connectivity.

3. **Gather information about the problem before contacting IT.** Write down or note:
   - What is broken or not working (specific application, feature, device, etc.)
   - When it started (this morning, after an update, etc.)
   - What error message you see (write it down exactly if possible)
   - How many people are affected (just you, your team, the whole office)
   - What have you already tried

4. **Check if others have the same problem.** Ask nearby colleagues or your team lead if they are experiencing the same issue. If multiple people are affected, this indicates a system-wide problem (not just your device), which IT will prioritize.

5. **Check the IT Self-Service Portal or company intranet for status updates.** Sometimes IT posts notices about known issues, service outages, or maintenance. Check there before contacting IT.

6. **Look for an error message or status indicator.** Many applications show error codes or specific messages. Take a screenshot or write down the exact error. This helps IT diagnose faster.

7. **Verify your internet connection is working.** Open a web browser and try accessing a website (like google.com or your company intranet). If the web works but a specific application does not, it is likely application-specific, not network-wide.

8. **Check if you are connected to VPN if working remotely.** If you work from home, ensure your VPN connection shows "Connected" in your VPN client. Reconnect if it shows "Disconnected" or "Connecting".

**How to submit a ticket or contact IT:**

1. **For immediate urgent issues (affecting multiple people, complete system outage, security incident):** Call the IT Helpdesk at the emergency number (check your company intranet or contact directory) and describe the issue. This gets IT's immediate attention.

2. **For standard issues requiring support within hours:** Submit a ticket through the IT Self-Service Portal:
   - Go to the portal and select "Create a New Ticket" or "Report an Issue"
   - Select the category that best matches your issue (Hardware, Software, Network, etc.)
   - Provide a clear title: "Example: Outlook crashes on startup"
   - In the description, write what you have gathered: what is broken, when it started, any error messages, what you have tried, and how many people are affected
   - Set the priority: Critical (cannot work at all), High (major feature not working), Medium (inconvenient but have workaround), Low (minor or cosmetic)

3. **Attach screenshots if available.** If you have photos or screenshots of error messages, attach them to your ticket.

4. **Include your contact information.** Provide a phone number and email where IT can reach you. Confirm IT can contact you even if your email is not working.

**What IT will do:**

- Acknowledge receipt of your ticket within 15–30 minutes
- Prioritize based on urgency and business impact
- Contact you with follow-up questions or troubleshooting steps
- For remote support, may request to view your screen or access your device (with your permission)
- Provide a solution, workaround, or escalation to specialist teams
- Keep you updated on progress until resolved

**Important notes:**

- Do not attempt to uninstall system software or drivers unless IT instructs you to do so
- Do not share your password, even with IT staff (IT will never ask for it)
- If you receive an email or message asking for sensitive information (password, PIN, credit card), mark it as phishing and report it to IT Security
- Document any changes you make (software installed, cables unplugged, settings changed) in case IT needs to know to help troubleshoot

**Example ticket:**
"Title: Outlook crashes immediately when launching
Description: Outlook application closes immediately after I click to open it. This started this morning after I restarted my computer. I see no error message, just a brief splash screen then it closes. I have tried restarting my computer and disabling my antivirus but Outlook still crashes. This affects only my device, not my team. I need email access to do my job. Phone: [your number]"
